# The Job Offer for Field Roles

The job is the offer. Applicants compare it against every other post they saw that night, the job they have now, and not working. Most service businesses lose at this stage before anyone reads the post.

Use `offers` for the general framework (value, certainty, speed, convenience). This file applies it to hourly and field hiring.

## What field workers actually weigh

In rough order, for cleaners, techs, installers, and crews:

1. **Predictable weekly take-home.** Not the hourly rate. A $17/hour job with 38 guaranteed hours beats a $20/hour job with "25–40 hours depending on bookings."
2. **Schedule they can plan around.** Set days, set start time, done by a known hour, weekends stated plainly.
3. **A boss who is not a nightmare.** They cannot see this from a post, so they read for signals: tone, respect, whether you explain things.
4. **A path.** Lead, trainer, supervisor, more per hour at a milestone. Stated with a number and a timeline.
5. **Not paying for the job.** Supplies, uniform, gas, vehicle, phone. Anything they currently cover out of pocket.
6. **Getting paid fast.** Weekly pay, or daily pay in the first week.

Pay rate is in all of these but never alone. A business at the 50th percentile on rate can win on hours, schedule, and pay cadence without raising the rate.

## Levers, cheapest first

| Lever | What it costs you | What it is worth to them |
|-------|-------------------|--------------------------|
| Guaranteed minimum hours | Scheduling discipline | Removes the biggest fear in hourly work |
| Weekly pay | Payroll cadence change | Cash flow relief, especially the first month |
| Paid training from hour one | A few hundred dollars | Signals you are not going to burn them |
| Set schedule, no weekends (or weekends stated) | Routing discipline | Planning childcare and a second job |
| Supplies, uniform, gas provided | Already paying for most of it | Feels like a raise |
| Named milestone raise at 90 days | A dollar an hour for people who stayed | A reason to get to day 90 |
| Referral bonus | Paid only on retained hires | Social proof that people stay |
| Same-day or daily pay in week one | A payroll tool fee | Gets them through the gap from their last job |
| Vehicle or ride to sites | Depends on model | Opens the pool to people without a reliable car |
| Flexible start (today, tomorrow, next week) | Nothing | Beats every competitor with a 2-week process |

Stack two or three. Lead the post with the one your competitors do not offer.

## Pay structure choices

**Hourly.** Simplest to explain. Pair with guaranteed hours or it reads as "part-time whenever."

**Per job or per route.** Common in cleaning. Attracts fast workers, repels careful ones unless quality is checked. State the realistic weekly number, not the best-case.

**Hourly plus performance.** Hourly base with a per-job or review-based bonus. Best of both when the bonus is simple and paid weekly.

**Commission or percentage.** Works for sales-adjacent roles only. Field workers read it as risk.

Whatever the structure, the post states one weekly number range. Applicants convert the rate to weekly in their heads anyway; do it for them.

## Writing the offer line

Formula: pay as weekly range + guaranteed hours + the one lever + start timing.

- "$700–$850/week. 32 hours guaranteed. Paid weekly. Start this week."
- "$22–$28/hr, 40 hours. Company van and gas. No on-call."
- "$18/hr, Mon–Fri, done by 4. Paid training from day one."

That line is the first thing in the post, the first text to applicants, and the first thing said in the phone screen. Everything else supports it.

## Guarantees and risk reversal for hiring

Applicants fear wasting their time: an unpaid working interview, a job that is not what was described, or a two-week process that goes nowhere.

- "Paid working interview. Two hours, paid at full rate, no obligation either way."
- "If the job isn't what we described in your first week, we'll pay you for the week and help you find something else."
- "You'll hear from us within 15 minutes of applying, 7 days a week."

Pick one. Put it in the post.

## Red flags in the current offer

- "Competitive pay" with no number
- "Must be reliable" as the first requirement
- "Hours vary" or "part-time to full-time"
- Unpaid training or trial
- Requirements that are preferences (own vehicle, 2 years experience) for roles you train anyway
- A 2-week hiring process for a role that could start Monday
- A referral bonus nobody has ever been paid

Each one is a reason an applicant takes the other job. Remove them before spending on traffic.
