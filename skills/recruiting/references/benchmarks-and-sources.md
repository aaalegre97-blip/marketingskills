# Benchmarks and Sources

The numbers behind the funnel benchmarks, with where they come from. Use this when an owner asks "says who?" or when a client's market differs from the default and you need to know which figures are firm and which are rules of thumb.

Figures are from published reports and vendor data as of 2026. Vendor data (Workstream, Fountain, Talview, GoHire, Pin, CloudApper) is directional: it comes from their customers and favors their product. Rows marked "rule of thumb" are operator conventions this skill uses, not measured benchmarks. Cross-industry reports (CareerPlug, Appcast, Shift Project) are firmer. Local pay data always beats any of this; check Indeed's salary tool and three competitor posts per market.

| Jobs with pay, schedule, and benefits listed | 50% more apply starts | Indeed employer resources |
| Application under 5 minutes vs 15+ minutes | 12.5% completion vs 3.6% | Pin application-length study, 2026 |
| Candidates who start but never finish an application | 92% (over half cite length or complexity) | SHRM, citing Appcast |
| Indeed cost per application | $15–50 typical; $5–8 for entry-level service roles; $25/day minimum per sponsored post reported from July 2025 (check current Indeed pricing) | Pin, Hiretruffle, PerfectlyHired pricing guides 2026 |
| Cost per applicant, all sources | $5–25 cleaner, $30–120 skilled trade | Rule of thumb |
| Pay at or above the local 60th percentile | Stage-1 "healthy" line | Rule of thumb |
| Indeed sponsored vs free | Sponsored jobs 3.5x more likely to result in a hire; free listings lose visibility as new posts arrive | Indeed employer resources |
| Reposting on Indeed | Duplicate or frequently reposted jobs rank lower; Indeed says to update the single active post, not create a new one | Indeed job posting rules and policies |

**What this changes in practice:** the skill's "healthy" click-to-apply is 6% or better, with 10%+ meaning the post is doing real work. Pay in the post is not optional. One-tap apply is not a nicety; it is the difference between 12% and 4% completion. Keep one post per role and edit it rather than reposting.

## Speed and response

| Metric | Figure | Source |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Hourly workers' expectation | 50% expect contact within 3 days; 11% within 24 hours | Candidate survey cited by HR Dive |
| Actual median employer response | 6.7 days | Candidate experience research cited by HR Dive |
| Hourly applicant behavior | Apply to several employers in one sitting and take the first credible response | Workstream hourly hiring guides |
| Text open rate | 98%, most within 3 minutes | Workstream, Netchex |
| Text vs email response rate | 45% vs 6% (some vendors report 60–70% for recruiting texts) | 4 Corner Resources, GoHire, Callhub |
| Follow-up within an hour of first contact | Contact rates rise sharply versus waiting a few hours | Recruiting News Network |
| Candidates who withdraw over poor communication alone | 47% | Candidate experience research, Pin |
| Employer ghosting | 53% of job seekers ghosted by an employer in the past year | The Interview Guys 2025 Ghosting Index |

**What this changes in practice:** the 15-minute text standard is aggressive by the survey numbers and correct by the behavioral ones. The median employer takes a week; the applicant has taken another job by then. Texting is the channel; email is a backup.

## Interviews and show rate

| Metric | Figure | Source |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Interview no-show without reminders | 18–25% in general hiring (Talview baseline); 30–50% in high-volume hourly hiring, hourly phone screens up to 38%, seasonal peaks far higher | Talview; CloudApper, Workstream, Workwolf |
| No-show with 24-hour and 1-hour reminders | Drops from 18–25% to 8–12% | Talview |
| Phone screen → interview booked 50%+, interview → offer 50%+ | Cleaning-playbook weekly targets | Rule of thumb |
| Interviews booked within 24 hours | 71–72% show rate (too fast to plan around) | Talview |
| Interviews booked 24–72 hours out | 90–92% show rate | Talview |
| Candidates who quit because scheduling took too long | 42%; 55% give up if no interview within a week | The Interview Guys, Pin |
| Automated scheduling | 67% less drop-off between screen and interview; one courier operation saw 70% fewer no-shows | Fountain, Talview |
| Cost of each no-show | About $500 in recruiter and manager time | CloudApper |
| Group interviews for cleaners | Recommended by ZenMaid: no-shows cost a slot, not a morning; pay and schedule covered once | ZenMaid Magazine |

**What this changes in practice:** book interviews 24 to 72 hours out, not same-day and not next week. Three text reminders. Automate the booking. A show rate of 80%+ is realistic with reminders; 60–75% is what you get without them.

## Working interviews and pay law

| Point | Detail | Source |
|-------|--------|--------|
| Working interviews are compensable | If the applicant does work the business benefits from, the FLSA requires at least minimum wage for those hours, whether or not they are hired | US DOL enforcement; HR Dive; The Employer Handbook |
| Enforcement example | A dental practice paid $50,000 in back wages and damages over unpaid working interviews | HR Dive |
| State law | Some states (New York among them) have stricter rules on trial shifts | Levy Ratner |

**What this changes in practice:** every working interview or trial shift is paid. The skill says to pay the full job rate because it also converts better, but minimum wage is the legal floor.

## Offer to start and early retention

| Metric | Figure | Source |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Accepted offer, never showed day 1 | About 1 in 5 | Fountain, citing a job-seeker survey |
| Employers reporting under 80% first-day attendance | 53% outside peak season | Workwolf |
| Day-1 show after automated reminders and background-check tracking | 68% to 95% at one warehouse employer | Fountain (Stitch Fix case) |
| New hires leaving within 90 days | 43% frontline; 30% in broader surveys | Enboarder, citing frontline data; Pin |
| When new hires decide | 29% know in the first week, 70% within the first month | Enboarder |
| Top reasons for leaving in 90 days | Job not as described (30%), no connection to the team (20%), poor onboarding (17%); for frontline: pay or hours differ from what was promised, no visible path, manager absent in the first days | Enboarder, Fountain, 5Starr |
| Structured onboarding | 69% more likely to stay three years; up to 82% better retention | Enboarder, Devlin Peck roundups citing SHRM and Glassdoor |
| 75% retained at day 30, 60% at day 90 (cleaning) | Stage-7 "healthy" line | Rule of thumb, set below the 43%-in-90-days frontline figure |
| Cost per retained hire under one week of that worker's billed revenue | Cleaning-playbook target | Rule of thumb |

**What this changes in practice:** the day-before text, a named trainer, and a day-3 and day-7 check-in are the cheapest retention program that exists. The first-paycheck walkthrough targets the single most-cited reason frontline hires quit.

## Pay structure, cadence, and schedule

| Metric | Figure | Source |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Same-day or earned-wage access | 79% of workers more interested in applying; 56% would stay longer; employers report 27% lower turnover and users staying 2–3x longer | DailyPay, Netchex, Rain, HBS working paper |
| Schedule instability | On-call shifts, cancellations, and short notice each significantly raise quits among hourly service workers; 66% want a more stable schedule | Shift Project (Harvard Kennedy School), Choper, Schneider and Harknett 2022 |
| Stable scheduling at The Gap | Median sales up 7%, labor productivity up 5% | University of Chicago / Shift Project |
| Cleaning pay models | Hourly rewards slowness, per-job rewards rushing; the best operators pair a base with a per-job or quality bonus and a real quality check | Gem City Cleaning Tools, Service Autopilot, Connecteam, BookingKoala |
| Cleaning pay floor | At least $2–3/hour above local minimum to hold reliable people | Connecteam, BookingKoala |

**What this changes in practice:** guaranteed hours and weekly or earned-wage pay are retention tools with evidence behind them, not perks. For cleaners, lead with hourly plus a simple bonus and pair it with a quality check.

## Referrals

| Metric | Figure | Source |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Retention, referred vs job-board hires | 46% vs 33% in one dataset; 42% vs 32% in another | Zippia, ClearCompany roundups |
| Tenure | Referred hires stay about 70% longer; 45% stay four-plus years vs 25% from job boards | Zippia, Chanty |
| Bonus structure | 50/50 is the most common split: half at start (paid within two weeks), half at 90 days. $500–2,000 for frontline roles at mid-size companies; small service businesses commonly use $100–500 | ERIN, Eddy, Underdog |

**What this changes in practice:** a referral program is the highest-retention source in the funnel. Pay the first half fast or staff stop referring.

## Technicians and trades

| Point | Detail | Source |
|-------|--------|--------|
| Supply | About 0.6 new entrants per retiring tradesperson | Talk24, citing trade workforce data |
| Who is on job boards | Early-career techs, people burned by a bad employer, adjacent-trade switchers. Experienced techs are employed and not looking | The Blue Collar Recruiter |
| What moves experienced techs | Direct outreach from the owner or a lead tech, referrals, schedule (four-day weeks, no on-call), a take-home truck, a visible path to lead or manager, paid licensing | ServiceTitan, BDR, The Blue Collar Recruiter |
| Process length | More than three interview rounds loses strong candidates to competing offers | ServiceTitan |

**What this changes in practice:** for trades, traffic is the weakest lever and the offer plus referrals are the strongest. Treat it as outbound recruiting, not job posting.

## Facebook and Instagram job ads

| Point | Detail | Source |
|-------|--------|--------|
| Targeting | Employment ads fall under Meta's special ad category: no targeting by age, gender, or narrow zip; use a radius and let the creative self-select | Boostpoint, Meta policy |
| Format | Leads objective with an Instant Form; one ad per role; 2–3 knockout questions in the form; higher-intent form type | Boostpoint, Wonderkind, Zapier |
| Creative | Pay, schedule, and the main requirement in the ad so the wrong people scroll past; real photos of staff | Boostpoint, Recruitmenthaus |

**What this changes in practice:** the ad does the screening, not the audience settings. Put the weekly pay and schedule in the first line of the ad, same as the job post.
